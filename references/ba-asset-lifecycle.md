## What Capital Asset Management covers (paraphrased summary — verify specifics against the live guide)

**Capital Asset Management (eoaca).** CAM is really an umbrella over several licensed sub-modules: Fixed Assets, Plant and Equipment Management (equipment info, equipment time billing, plant/equipment maintenance, work-order foundation, failure analysis, case management, maintenance planning), Condition-Based Maintenance, Equipment Cost Analysis, and Resource Assignments — which one(s) a given org uses depends on licensing. The equipment master (Asset Master F1201 + Equipment Master Extension F1217) is the shared record: it's set up once and read by every one of those sub-modules, which keeps equipment data consistent instead of re-entered per system. Equipment identification is built from four pieces of ID information, and equipment can be organized into parent/component relationships (e.g. a ventilation fan with a motor as a trackable, inventory-linked component) — components can carry their own preventive-maintenance schedules and parts lists.

Setup sequence: CAM UDCs and constants → next numbers for equipment → product family/model combinations → customer and service-provider records → equipment master (fixed-asset range of accounts, journal entries for equipment costs, specification and supplemental data) → equipment billing (system integration, billing setup, charges for equipment use) → resource planning (work centers, work-center rates, available resource-unit calculation). If migrating from legacy Plant and Equipment Management into CAM, or bringing existing Fixed Assets records under CAM, a one-time equipment/work-order extension-record batch update is required before the newer CAM programs (which need both the base table and its CAM extension table, e.g. F1201+F1217 for equipment, F4801+F4801T for work orders) will function correctly.

Business value framing Oracle uses: CAM gives visibility to schedule servicing around production, lets planners spot wear patterns from repair history to plan replacements before failures cause downtime, and ties maintenance labor/parts cost control into the same cash-flow view used for asset deployment/retirement decisions.

Closely related: **Equipment Cost Analysis** and **Condition-Based Maintenance** guides (separate implementation guides, same asset-lifecycle folder) extend CAM's cost-tracking and condition-triggered maintenance respectively; **Work Orders Implementation Guide** (shared with Supply Chain & Manufacturing) covers the work-order mechanics CAM maintenance tasks run on top of; **Fixed Assets** (topic `ba-financials`) covers the GL/depreciation side of the same Asset Master records.
