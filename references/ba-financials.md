## What each guide covers (paraphrased summary — verify specifics against the live guide)

**Accounts Payable (eoaap).** Manages supplier vouchers, matching, and payments. Setup centers on AP constants (batch control, offset-entry method, duplicate-invoice checking), UDCs (voucher/matching document types, payment status and instrument codes), and AAI items starting with "P" (PC = payables trade account, PB = payables bank account) that tell the system which GL accounts to hit when a voucher posts. Vouchers can be entered directly, logged (received before matching), or generated automatically from procurement receipts (voucher match, two-way or three-way). Delivered workflow includes batch approval (a secured user's voucher batch stays "pending" until an approver releases it) and a separate voucher-approval workflow keyed off gross-amount thresholds that holds pay status until approved. Integrates with Procurement (vouchers from PO matching), Fixed Assets/CAM (asset acquisition/maintenance vouchers), Expense Management, Payroll (tax/benefit liability vouchers), Job Cost/Subcontracts, and Real Estate Management. 1099 processing and country-specific localizations (e.g. Argentina, Brazil, Italy, Japan, UK, Poland) layer on top of the base AP setup.

**Accounts Receivable (eoaar).** Manages customer invoicing, receipts application, and credit/collections. Setup includes AR constants (enhanced/system/company-level), UDCs (document/invoice/receipt types, payment status, credit/collection manager codes, aging vocabulary, chargeback reason codes), and record-reservation locking to prevent concurrent-update conflicts on high-volume AR data. Core business flow: invoice → apply receipt (domestic, foreign, or alternate currency; general-ledger or balance-forward method) → age/dun as needed. Integrates with Sales Order Management (invoices from sales), General Accounting (GL offset via AAIs), and Multicurrency Processing.

**Financial Management Fundamentals (eoaff).** The shared foundation every other Financials guide leans on. Covers: batch approval/post security (management approval by system, secured-user setup); organizations (fiscal date patterns, companies, business units, organizational structures); next numbers (by system, and by company/fiscal year); the chart of accounts (business unit.object.subsidiary format, category codes, level of detail, posting edit codes, model/alternate chart of accounts, third GL account numbers); and AAIs — the rule tables that map day-to-day transactions (vouchers, invoices, receipts, depreciation, etc.) to the correct GL accounts without the user manually specifying an offset account each time.

**General Accounting (eoaga).** The GL engine: journal entry, batch posting, account reconciliation. Implementation sequence typically follows Fundamentals (chart of accounts) → GA constants → multicurrency setup → ledger type rules → address book → GA-specific UDCs, AAIs, and business-unit supplemental data → next numbers → batch approval/post security → account reconciliation setup.

**Fixed Assets (eoafa).** Tracks the accounting lifecycle of capital assets — add, depreciate, revalue, split, transfer, dispose. Key concepts: account rules (link cost accounts to their depreciation-expense/accumulated-depreciation accounts) and depreciation rules (the algorithm applied to an asset's cost over its life); an asset can carry multiple depreciation "books" simultaneously (e.g. a 3-year tax life vs. a 5-year book life, or separate books per currency). Splits/transfers/disposals auto-generate the matching journal entries and update location/subledger records. Country-specific fixed-asset functionality exists for Chile, Colombia, Peru, and Russia. Shares core tables (Asset Master F1201, etc.) with Capital Asset Management — see topic `ba-asset-lifecycle`.

**Advanced Cost Accounting (eoaac).** Managerial/activity-based costing layered on top of standard GL data — used for profitability and "what-if" analysis (e.g. showing that identical sales volume can carry very different margins once shipment size, packaging, or special handling are factored in). Captures data in a cost analyzer table, supports both traditional cost accounting and activity-based costing (which, unlike GAAP-based accounting, doesn't require debits to equal credits), and can combine the two for flexibility.

**Multicurrency Processing (eoamp).** Enables working in an unlimited number of currencies with automatic conversion between domestic, foreign, and alternate currencies. Basic setup: activate multicurrency on GA constants (multiplier "Y" or divisor "Z" conversion method), designate currency codes, set each company's domestic currency (and whether to post account balances by currency / enable detailed currency restatement), then flag currency-specific accounts and set up exchange rates (updated regularly for realized/unrealized gain-loss calculation). Same transaction-entry programs are used for domestic and foreign currency — the multicurrency flag drives the behavior underneath.

**Tax Processing (eoatp).** Cross-product tax engine used by AP, AR, GA, and Procurement. Central concept is the tax explanation code (S = sales tax, U = use tax, V = VAT, C = VAT+sales tax, B = VAT+use tax, E = exempt, etc. — user-definable via UDC 00/EX) which controls how a tax is assessed and how it posts to GL revenue/expense accounts; behavior differs depending on whether an item is purchased for stock and whether two-way or three-way voucher match is used. Setup covers tax authorities, tax UDCs, tax rules by company, tax AAIs (hierarchy of PTxxxx/RTxxxx/GTxxxx items for AP/AR/GA, plus procurement-specific PTVATD), and tax rate areas (stored in F4008, non-overlapping effective-date ranges). U.S./Canadian tax rates are typically sourced from the Vertex Sales Tax Q Series integration rather than set up manually.

**Joint Venture Management (eoajv).** For entities that share costs/revenue on a joint venture. The managing partner defines the JV as a legal entity, sets each partner's Division of Interest (DOI, date-effective), and the system distributes actual expenses/revenue/overhead pro rata based on DOI — generating invoices (for capital-cost/expense-range accounts), vouchers (for revenue-range accounts), or journal entries (for "insider" business units) to bill or pay each partner. Supports cash calls (drawing funds from partners ahead of billing), overhead allocation/calculation, and automatic approve-and-post of JV-specific batch types (bypassing the normal GA/AP/AR approval hierarchy if configured that way). Delivered as part of the Financials module (no separate SKU, no Tools-release dependency, though UX One Analytics needs Tools 9.2.2) — works alongside Job Cost, Real Estate Management, or Grower Management depending on industry.

**Expense Management (system code 09E, eoaem).** Employee expense-report entry, audit, and reimbursement. Policies define expense categories (e.g. airfare) with per diem/daily-allowance rules, receipt requirements, and audit thresholds; amounts over the allowance post as "unallowable" to a separate object/subsidiary account. Group and employee profiles set reimbursement method/currency and whether manager approval is required (overridable per employee). Flow: submit → manager approve/reject → auditor accept/reject → reimbursement run (R20110), which generates AP voucher or Payroll timecard records depending on the configured reimbursement method.

**1099 Year-End Processing, Base Currency Conversion, Sustainability Framework, Financial Management Reports, One View Financial Statements** — narrower/statutory-flavored guides not deep-dived here; fetch the index page or `web_search` the guide title directly for anything specific (1099 = annual US 1099-MISC/NEC reporting cycle; Base Currency Conversion = one-time conversion of a company's base/domestic currency, e.g. euro changeovers; Sustainability Framework = emissions/ESG data capture that also appears on the Health/Safety/Environment guide list; the two Reports/One View guides are largely catalogs of standard reports rather than configuration guides).
